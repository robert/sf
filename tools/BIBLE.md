# THE GREAT BRIDGE ROBBERY — story bible

A daily bedtime story website a father wrote for his children while on a business trip to San Francisco (22 Oct – 1 Nov 2026). One chapter unlocks per day (11 chapters). Every chapter exists in TWO versions of the SAME events:

- **samuel** version — for Samuel, age 4, read ALOUD by a grown-up. 1–2 minutes aloud = **230–320 words**. Short sentences, vivid sound effects (ZZZIP! CREEEAK! WHOOSH!), a repeated refrain or two, funny bits, lots of dialogue, warm. Simple words, but don't be babyish — 4-year-olds love big funny words now and then. Gentle peril only.
- **oscar** version — for Oscar, age 7, very bright, reads like an 11-year-old, reading to himself. 5 minutes silent reading = **1000–1300 words**. Rich vocabulary, wit, proper chapter-book prose (think Roald Dahl / Lemony Snicket's warmth / Cressida Cowell), genuine tension, clever deductions, real San Francisco facts woven in naturally (see facts list). Can be more complex in plot detail, but the events are the same as Samuel's version.

Both versions end on a **cliffhanger** (except Ch 11, which ends warmly) and then a **puzzle** (riddle / logic puzzle) drawn from that chapter's story. Samuel's puzzle must be solvable by a 4-year-old with a bit of help (counting, colours, "which one is different", simple riddle with a clear answer). Oscar's puzzle should be a real challenge for a very smart 7-year-old (logic grids, liar/truth-teller, sequences, lateral-thinking riddles) — but fair and with ONE clear correct answer. Answers include a one- or two-sentence explanation.

## Characters

- **Daddy** — the narrator's subject (third person: "Daddy"). A computer programmer on a business trip. Does NOT wear glasses. Kind, funny, and a hopeless WORRIER: "Oh dear, oh dear, oh dear." Constantly fretting about his meetings: "Oh dear, I've got a meeting at nine!" He is the cautious one; the boys are bold and brave. He sometimes says nerdy programmer things. In Ch 10 he finally gets HIS brave moment, using programming to save the day.
- **Oscar** (7, he/him) — clever, calm, a big reader (has read about everything; quotes facts from books; notices tiny written clues) and loves FOOTBALL (always has a football; can kick with amazing accuracy — this matters in Ch 10). The thinker/detective.
- **Samuel Lightning Thunderbolt Fire The Knight** (4, he/him) — insists on his FULL title (use it with relish, especially when he announces himself; otherwise "Samuel" or "Sam"). Loves KNIGHTS and DINOSAURS. Carries a wooden sword and wears a colander as a helmet; has a toy T. rex called **Roary**. Fearless, loud, charges in. Says things like "FOR THE KING!" and "ROOOAR!"
- **Mummy** and **Rosie** (baby sister) — back home in England. Video/phone calls. Rosie is a baby and CANNOT TALK: she babbles ("BA! GOO!"), squeals and points. Mummy does NOT know about the stowaways at first (in Ch 1 she thinks the boys are tucked up in bed / off at a sleepover-type mix-up — keep it light and plausible: Daddy leaves before dawn while everyone's asleep). She FINDS OUT on the video call in Ch 2, shortly after landing: Daddy tries to hide the boys, Rosie gives them away, Samuel bursts out. Mummy is astonished and briefly very cross ("DADDY!") — then, seeing they're safe and there's a mystery, she decides the boys can stay until the trip ends, on the strict condition that they LOOK AFTER DADDY and call every day. From then on Mummy (and Victoria) are in on it, cheering them on during later calls, and Daddy is slightly terrified of Mummy's opinion. Ch 11 homecoming: a big hug; Mummy: "Next time, you're all taking ME."

- **Victoria** — the family's nanny (does NOT live with the family; she's there in the daytime), with Mummy and Rosie. Just call her "Victoria" (never "Victoria the nanny"). Fun, unflappable, sometimes appears on the video calls holding Rosie, and is perhaps the first to suspect where the boys are (she finds out with Mummy in Ch 2 and is unflappable about it — 'I'll pack them some snacks next time'). Feature her naturally in the home/phone-call scenes (Ch 1 departure, Ch 2 call where she and Mummy find out, and at least one or two other calls across the story, e.g. a quick call in Ch 5 or 7, and Ch 11 homecoming).
- **Delphine Okafor** (always called just "Delphine" — no Ms./Mrs. titles for anyone) — manager of the hotel, very tall, elegant, frantic about the missing bridge (the hotel's famous "Bridge View Suites" now look out on nothing).
- **The hotel**: The Grand Magnolia Hotel, on top of Nob Hill. Daddy is in room 1937 (a coincidence that matters — it's the year the bridge opened).
- **Brenda** (Brenda Henderson; always called "Brenda", never "Brenda") — Daddy's boss, on video calls. Very serious. Says "Let's circle back."
- **Big Lou** — cable car gripman, enormous moustache, rings the bell in rhythms.
- **May Wu** (called "May") — runs a fortune cookie bakery in Chinatown. Sharp-eyed.
- **The Admiral** — an enormous old sea lion at Pier 39 who only talks to children (Daddy just hears "ARK ARK"). Grumpy, grand, smells of fish.
- **Professor Pemberton Pinch** — the thief. Long purple coat, tiny round spectacles (he's the only one with glasses), a voice like a creaky door. "Collector of Famous Things." Has a glowing gadget: the **Shrink-u-lator**. Lives secretly in a hidden part of Alcatraz Island. NOT truly wicked — lonely: he collects the world's most beautiful things because nobody ever comes to visit him. Redeemed at the end.

## Same story, two tellings

The two versions MUST tell exactly the same story — same events, same order, same jokes, same clues, same cliffhanger. Only the telling differs (length, vocabulary, detail). The puzzle may differ in difficulty but should be about the same thing.

## Tone

Elegant, funny, warm, with real wonder. Daddy's worrying is a running gag ("Oh dear, oh dear, oh dear"). The boys' bravery is the heart. Keep it safe-feeling: nobody is ever really hurt. British English spelling and phrasing (Mummy, colour, pavement, holiday) but Americans in SF talk American.

## Real SF facts to use (Oscar version especially)

Golden Gate Bridge opened in 1937; painted "International Orange"; about 1.7 miles (2.7 km) long; towers 746 feet tall; its cables contain enough wire to wrap round the Earth three times; it's often hidden by fog (which locals call "Karl"). Cable cars: since 1873, the only moving-by-hand cable car system in the world; pulled by a cable running under the street; the gripman pulls a lever ("the grip"); bells; there's a bell-ringing contest. Nob Hill is steep. Chinatown is the oldest in North America; the fortune cookie was likely popularised in San Francisco (Japanese Tea Garden, early 1900s). Pier 39 sea lions arrived after the 1989 earthquake and stayed; they bark and flop on wooden floats. Alcatraz: island prison "The Rock", closed 1963, 1.25 miles offshore, cold currents, now a park, lots of seabirds. Lombard Street: the crooked street. Hills, steep streets, hilly views, Victorian painted houses ("Painted Ladies").

## Running gag: the stand-in meetings

Samuel stands on Oscar's shoulders inside Daddy's long coat, with Daddy's hat, to "be Daddy" in meetings while Daddy investigates. They speak random business/programmer jargon: "We need to refactor the synergy pipeline." "Let's deploy to the cloud. The fluffy one." "Have you tried turning it off and on again?" "I propose we leverage the dinosaurs." Brenda is always delighted. These are among the funniest scenes.

## Chapter beats (BOTH versions follow these; same events)

1. **22 Oct — The Suspiciously Heavy Suitcase.** Daddy packs for SF, says goodbye to Mummy and Rosie (Daddy leaves before dawn while the house sleeps; Mummy and Victoria have no idea). Two suitcases are incredibly heavy. The plane. Taxi up the steep hill to The Grand Magnolia, room 1937. Suitcases giggle. ZZZZIP — Oscar (with football) and Samuel Lightning Thunderbolt Fire The Knight (colander helmet, sword, Roary) pop out. Daddy: "Oh dear, oh dear, oh dear!" They can't go home (no tickets!), they must stay. Jet-lagged, they sleep. In the morning, Daddy opens the curtains to see the famous Golden Gate Bridge... and it is GONE. Just two empty cliffs, seagulls and a confused traffic jam. CLIFFHANGER. Puzzle: riddle about a bridge/suitcase (e.g. Samuel: "I have a zip but I'm not a coat... what am I?" ; Oscar: something harder).
2. **23 Oct — Hide From Mummy!** Video call with Mummy and Rosie at breakfast. The boys hide behind curtains / under the bed. Rosie keeps pointing: "Ossa! Sam-sam!" Near-miss comedy, then Rosie gives them away and Samuel bursts out: Mummy FINDS OUT. Astonishment, crossness, then agreement: they can stay if they look after Daddy. The TV news: the Golden Gate Bridge is officially MISSING. Police baffled. Delphine frantic. While Daddy frets about his meeting, Oscar notices something on the windowsill / balcony: a tiny red-orange bolt, the size of a grain of rice. CLIFFHANGER. Puzzle about where it came from / counting / colours.
3. **24 Oct — The Nine O'Clock Meeting.** Daddy needs to investigate the bolt but has a 9am video meeting with Brenda. The boys do it for him (Samuel on Oscar's shoulders in Daddy's coat). Hilarious jargon. Brenda loves it: "Best meeting all quarter." Meanwhile Daddy borrows a magnifying glass from the hotel gift shop. Oscar reads the stamp on the tiny bolt: "GG 1937". Oscar deduces: the bridge wasn't taken away — it was SHRUNK. CLIFFHANGER: and Brenda wants a follow-up meeting "with the tall version of you" next week. Puzzle.
4. **25 Oct — The Cable Car Clue.** They ride a cable car down Nob Hill. Big Lou the gripman, who was oiling the cable car late on the night of the robbery, saw a man in a long purple coat carrying a glowing gadget and something long and red "like a toy bridge", heading for the bay. Three passengers also claim to have seen something; one is fibbing. Big Lou rings his bell in a strange rhythm. Daddy frets the whole way. CLIFFHANGER: in the cable car seat, Samuel finds a crumpled paper bag from a fortune cookie bakery. Puzzle: liar/truth puzzle (Oscar) / simple "who is telling the truth" or counting bells (Samuel).
5. **26 Oct — Fortune Cookies.** Chinatown — lanterns, the smell of baking. May's bakery. She remembers a strange customer: a purple coat who ordered 500 fortune cookies, all with the same fortune. Order slip: "Professor P. Pinch, Collector of Famous Things. Deliver to: Pier 39, the old rowing boat." Samuel eats too many cookies. CLIFFHANGER: Samuel cracks a cookie and the fortune says: "Little knight, the bridge is not the only thing he has taken." Puzzle: choose the true fortune of three (Oscar logic) / simple riddle (Samuel).
6. **27 Oct — The Admiral.** Pier 39, barking sea lions. The Admiral, a vast grumpy sea lion, talks — but only to children (to Daddy it's "ARK ARK" — he's baffled). The Admiral saw the purple coat row out at midnight to Alcatraz, The Rock, with something glowing. The rowing boat is gone. He warns of the cold currents. Oscar asks how to get there. CLIFFHANGER: the Admiral says, "Oh, he'll be expecting you." Riddle: how to cross water without a bridge, etc.
7. **28 Oct — The Big Presentation.** Daddy's huge quarterly presentation clashes with planning the rescue. The boys do it (in the coat). Samuel's slide is a drawing of a dinosaur knight. Oscar explains "the offside rule of software". The whole board claps. Daddy, meanwhile, hires a little rowing boat (from a friendly fisherman), checks tides, packs torches. Night falls; fog-less moon. The three row out into the dark bay toward Alcatraz, Daddy saying "oh dear" with every stroke. CLIFFHANGER: a light flickers on in a window of the old prison. Puzzle.
8. **29 Oct — The Cabinet of Famous Things.** They sneak into Alcatraz. A secret door. A vast hall of glass cabinets: Big Ben the size of a pencil, the Eiffel Tower the size of a teaspoon, the Leaning Tower of Pisa (still leaning), a T. rex skeleton from a museum the size of a mouse, and — on a velvet cushion — the Golden Gate Bridge, small enough to step over. Professor Pinch appears ("Visitors? Nobody EVER visits"). He's flustered and grabs the glowing Shrink-u-lator. Samuel charges: "FOR THE KING!" — ZAP! CLIFFHANGER: Samuel Lightning Thunderbolt Fire The Knight is suddenly the size of a mouse. Puzzle.
9. **30 Oct — Very Small Samuel.** Daddy is beside himself. Tiny Samuel is utterly unafraid: he climbs onto the tiny T. rex skeleton (which, to him, is the right size to ride — a knight on a dinosaur!), dashes through pipes and under doors, past Professor Pinch's grumpy cat, to steal the brass key that unlocks the Shrink-u-lator's control panel. Oscar keeps Pinch talking about his collection (Oscar has read about all these things — Pinch is delighted to have someone interested). Samuel returns with the key. CLIFFHANGER: The control panel has three switches; the wrong one could shrink them ALL. Puzzle: three switches logic (Oscar) / which switch (Samuel, colours/shapes).
10. **31 Oct (Halloween) — Halloween Code.** They choose the right switch (the answer from Ch 9). But the Shrink-u-lator only shrinks — it can't un-shrink. Daddy looks at the glowing screen of code. His hands stop shaking. "Oh dear... oh. Oh! There's a bug." A minus sign in the wrong place. He fixes it: DAD'S BRAVE MOMENT. Professor Pinch lunges for the machine — Oscar's perfect football kick knocks the Shrink-u-lator out of his hands into Daddy's. Daddy un-shrinks Samuel (POP!) then everyone carries the tiny bridge outside. Pinch sinks down and admits, crying, that he was lonely — nobody ever visited. CLIFFHANGER/QUESTION: the bridge has to go back before sunrise... and what should happen to the Professor? Puzzle.
11. **1 Nov — Home Again.** At dawn the Shrink-u-lator ray un-shrinks the Golden Gate Bridge right back where it belongs, glowing International Orange in the sunrise. The other landmarks are sent home too. The city cheers; a parade; Delphine weeps with joy; Big Lou rings the bell; the Admiral barks. Professor Pinch opens his collection (of ordinary, wonderful things) as a museum on Pier 39 that everyone visits; he is never lonely again. Daddy's last meeting: Brenda promotes "the tall version" of him. Time to fly home: the boys climb back into the suitcases. At home, Mummy unzips the suitcases and isn't surprised in the least. Big hugs. Mummy: "Next time, you're all taking ME." And Rosie, in her cot, is holding something tiny and orange... a little souvenir model of the bridge? (Gentle mystery wink.) Final warm ending + a last riddle. THE END.
